    - Introduction to IELTS WRITING TASK 1
        - Content
            
            The **IELTS Writing Task 1** can feel a bit like a math test hiding inside an English test. Since you are starting from scratch, here is the essential breakdown of what it is, how it’s graded, and how to conquer it.
            
            ## 1. The Logistics
            
            - **Time:** You should spend about **20 minutes** on this task.
            - **Length:** You must write at least **150 words**.
            - **Weight:** It counts for **1/3** of your total Writing score (Task 2 is worth the other 2/3).
            - **Format:** You are given visual information (a chart, table, or diagram) and must describe it in your own words.
            
            ## 2. The Task Types
            
            You won't know which one you'll get until you open the booklet, but it will be one of these:
            
            1. **Line Graph:** Shows changes over time.
            2. **Bar Chart:** Compares different categories.
            3. **Pie Chart:** Shows shares of a whole (percentages).
            4. **Table:** A grid of data/numbers.
            5. **Process Diagram:** Shows how something is made or how a system works (e.g., the life cycle of a frog or how a factory works).
            6. **Map:** Shows changes in a location over two different time periods.
            7. **Mixed Task:** Two different charts (e.g., a bar chart and a pie chart) that you must compare.

            ## 3. The "Golden" Structure
            
            To get a high score, don't just write a giant block of text. Follow this four-paragraph formula:
            
            - **Paragraph 1: Introduction**
                - One sentence. Paraphrase the prompt (change the words, but keep the meaning).
                - *Example:* If the prompt says "The graph shows the number of cars sold," you write "The line graph illustrates the volume of vehicle sales."
            - **Paragraph 2: Overview (The most important part!)**
                - Two sentences. Summarize the **main trends** or most noticeable features without using specific numbers. If you skip this, it is very hard to get above a Band 5.
            - **Paragraph 3: Detail Group 1**
                - Focus on one specific part of the data. Use actual numbers and dates here.
            - **Paragraph 4: Detail Group 2**
                - Focus on the remaining data. Make comparisons (e.g., "In contrast to X, Y was significantly higher").
            
            ## 4. How You Are Graded
            
            The examiner looks at four specific areas (25% each):
            
            1. **Task Achievement:** Did you cover all the requirements? Did you include an overview?
            2. **Coherence and Cohesion:** Is it easy to read? Did you use linking words like *however, in addition,* and *subsequently*?
            3. **Lexical Resource:** Did you use a wide range of vocabulary (e.g., instead of "went up," did you use "surged," "rose," or "increased")?
            4. **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Did you use a mix of simple and complex sentences? Are your tenses correct?
            
            Check out this for more information: https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/ielts_writing_band_descriptors.pdf
            
            ## 5. Three Critical "Don'ts"
            
            - **Don't give your opinion:** Never explain *why* you think the data changed. If sales went down, don't say "because of the economy." Just state that they went down.
            - **Don't list every single number:** You are being tested on your ability to **summarize**. Pick the highest, lowest, and most important turning points.
            - **Don't copy the prompt word-for-word:** You will receive zero marks for any words copied directly from the question.

            - Task Achievement
                
                **Step 1: Identify Key Features (Data Analysis)**
                Before you start writing, spend 2–3 minutes scanning the chart to pinpoint these critical factors:
                
                - **The Extremes:**
                    - **highest** figures
                    - **lowest** figures
                - **The Movements:**
                    - **most drastic changes** (sharp rises or steep drops)
                    - **stability** (flat lines or plateaus)
                - **The Comparisons:**
                    - **similar patterns** (e.g. two countries trending upward together)
                    - **opposite patterns** (e.g. one rises while the other falls)?
                
                **Step 2: Overview** 
                
                The Comparisons + The Movements + The Extremes
                
                *Rule of thumb: Never include specific numbers or data points in this paragraph.*
                
                #### Example
                
                Overall, there was an increase in the proportion of female members in parliament in all five countries examined, with the most dramatic rise being seen in the figures for the UK. Despite this, the UK consistently had the lowest figures over the period.
                
                
                **Why this is a high-band example:**
                
                - It identifies the ****the comparisons (*"an increase in all five countries")*
                - It highlights the movements (*"most dramatic rise... in the UK"*)
                - It points out the extremes (*"consistently had the lowest figures"*)

                A strong overview acts as a bridge between the introduction and the data. To write a high-scoring overview, aim to combine a **contrast** (difference) with a **similarity**.
                
                - Similarities: “both”, “also”
                - Differences: “while”, “but”
                
                Example:
                
                Overall, while boys generally enjoyed outdoor activities the most, girls tended to join indoor ones. it is also clear that dramatic differences are not seen in the figures for teenagers of both sexes playing computer games and watching DVDs.

            - Lexical Resource
                
                
                | **Category** | **Verbs & Phrases (The "What Happened")** | **Context / Usage** |
                | --- | --- | --- |
                | **New Construction** | **Was built**, constructed, erected, established, introduced, added. | Buildings, facilities, or infrastructure. |
                | **Removal** | **Was demolished**, knocked down, pulled down, cleared, removed. | To make room for something else. |
                | **Transformation** | **Was converted into**, turned into, transformed, redeveloped, replaced by. | When the function of a building changes (e.g., factory to flats). |
                | **Growth/Expansion** | **Was extended**, widened, expanded, modernized, enlarged. | Roads, railways, or existing buildings. |
                | **Relocation** | **Was moved to**, relocated to, reconstructed on [site]. | Moving a structure from point A to point B. |
                | **No Change** | **Remained**, stayed the same, was left unaltered, no changes were made. | Used to show stability in an area. |
                
                #### Specific Categories for Nature and Infrastructure
                
                Sometimes general "change" words don't fit perfectly. Use these specialized terms for a more natural flow:
                
                #### 1. Trees and Greenery
                
                - **Removal:** Chopped down, cut down, cleared to make way for.
                - **Addition:** Planted, forested, landscaped.
                - **Negative Impact:** *The amenity* was built **at the expense of** the green area.
                
                #### 2. Transportation (Roads/Rail/Bridge)
                
                - **Connection:** Joined, linked, branched off (from).
                - **Alteration:** Diverted, deviated, reopened, modernized.
                
                #### 3. Describing Land State
                
                - **Before Development:** Bare land, vacant plot, open space, underdeveloped.
                - **After Development:** Industrialized, residentialized, urbanized.

                | **Section / Category** | **Sentence Structure & Key Phrases** | **Example** |
                | --- | --- | --- |
                | **Introduction** | **The [diagram / picture] [shows / describes / illustrates]** how [S + V] **OR** the process of [V-ing]. | "The diagram illustrates **how a plastic bottle is recycled** into new products." |
                | **Overview** | **Overall, the process comprises a number of steps, beginning with [A] and ending with [B].** | "Overall, the process comprises seven steps, **beginning with the collection of waste and ending with the distribution of final goods.**" |
                | **Overview** | ...from [Step 1] to [Step 2] to [Step 3]. | "The process moves **from sorting to melting to the final molding stage.**" |
                | **Body: Start** | **At the beginning of the process,** [S + V], after which... | "**At the beginning of the process**, the raw timber is delivered to the factory, **after which** it is cut into planks." |
                | **Body: Middle** | **The process continues with...** / **Next,** ... / **Then,** ... / **Subsequently,** ... | "**Subsequently**, the purified water is diverted into large holding tanks for cooling." |
                | **Body: Ending** | **Finally,** / **At the final stage of the process,** [S + V]. | "**Finally**, the finished products are packaged and loaded onto trucks for delivery." |
                | **Sequencing** | **Before [V-ing / S+V], [S+V].** | "**Before being heated**, the chemicals are mixed in a large vat." |
                | **Sequencing** | **After [Noun / S+V], [S+V].** | "**After the mixture has cooled**, it is poured into individual glass jars." |
                | **Sequencing** | ..., **which is followed by** [Noun / V-ing]. | "The fruit is washed thoroughly, **which is followed by** the removal of the seeds." |
                | **Sequencing** | **Once [S + V], [S + V].** | "**Once the clay is dry**, it is placed inside a high-temperature kiln." |
                | **Sequencing** | ..., **after which** [S + V]. | "The beans are roasted at 200°C, **after which** they are ground into a fine powder." |
                | **Sequencing** | ..., **at which point,** [S + V]. | "The liquid reaches its boiling point, **at which point** the steam is captured by a condenser." |
                | **Sequencing** | ..., **and then** [S + V]. | "The metal is melted **and then** it is poured into a pre-shaped mold." |
                | **Purpose** | **In order to...** / **So that...** / **To ensure that...** | "The air is filtered **to ensure that** no dust particles contaminate the final product." |
                | **Grammar Note** | **Passive Voice:** [Object] + **be + V3/Past Participle**. | "The glass **is crushed** into small pieces before it **is melted**." |
