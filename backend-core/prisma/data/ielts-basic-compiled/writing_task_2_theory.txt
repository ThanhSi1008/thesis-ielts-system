- writing_task_2 + theory
    - Introduction to Writing Task 2
        - Content
            ## 1. The Logistics
            
            - **Time:** 40 minutes (spend more time on Task 2 than Task 1)
            - **Length:** At least **250 words**
            - **Weight:** 2/3 of your total Writing score (Task 1 is only 1/3)
            - **Format:** You are given a statement or question about a topic. You must write an essay responding to it.
            
            ## 2. The 5 Question Types
            
            1. **Opinion Essay (Agree/Disagree):** "To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
            2. **Discussion Essay (Both Views):** "Discuss both views and give your own opinion."
            3. **Problem & Solution:** "What are the problems? What solutions can you suggest?"
            4. **Advantages & Disadvantages:** "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"
            5. **Two-Part Question:** "Why is this happening? Is this a positive or negative development?"
            
            ## 3. The Golden Structure (4 paragraphs)
            
            - **Paragraph 1: Introduction** (2-3 sentences)
                - Paraphrase the question
                - State your thesis/position clearly
            - **Paragraph 2: Body 1** (5-7 sentences)
                - Main argument/view/cause with examples
            - **Paragraph 3: Body 2** (5-7 sentences)
                - Second argument/counter-view/solution with examples
            - **Paragraph 4: Conclusion** (2-3 sentences)
                - Summarize your main points
                - Restate your opinion
            
            ## 4. How You Are Graded (same 4 criteria as Task 1)
            
            1. **Task Achievement (Task Response):** Did you address ALL parts of the question? Is your position clear throughout?
            2. **Coherence and Cohesion:** Is the essay logically organized? Do paragraphs flow naturally?
            3. **Lexical Resource:** Did you use topic-specific vocabulary accurately?
            4. **Grammatical Range and Accuracy:** Did you use complex sentence structures correctly?
            
            ## 5. Critical Don'ts
            
            - **Don't be vague:** Take a clear position. "I somewhat agree" is weak.
            - **Don't repeat the question word-for-word:** Paraphrase it.
            - **Don't write a memorized essay:** The examiner can tell.
            - **Don't forget the conclusion:** An incomplete essay cannot score above Band 5.
            - **Don't write fewer than 250 words:** You WILL be penalized.
            
            - Task Achievement
                | Part | What to Write |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introduction | Paraphrase the question + clear thesis statement |
                | Body 1 | First main idea + explanation + example |
                | Body 2 | Second main idea + explanation + example |
                | Conclusion | Summary + restate opinion (no new ideas) |
                
                **The thesis statement is the most important sentence in the essay.**
                
                Example thesis for "Agree/Disagree":
                - "I strongly agree that governments should invest more in public transportation."
                - "While some may argue otherwise, I firmly believe that the benefits of remote work far outweigh its drawbacks."
            - Grammar + Coherence & Cohesion
                | **Function** | **Phrases** |
                | --- | --- |
                | **Stating opinion** | I firmly believe / I strongly agree / In my view / From my perspective |
                | **Adding arguments** | Furthermore / Moreover / In addition / What is more |
                | **Contrasting** | However / On the other hand / Nevertheless / Conversely |
                | **Giving examples** | For instance / For example / A case in point is / To illustrate |
                | **Cause & Effect** | Consequently / As a result / Therefore / This leads to |
                | **Conceding** | Although / While it is true that / Despite / Admittedly |
                | **Concluding** | In conclusion / To sum up / Overall / Taking everything into account |
            - Lexical Resource

                | **Topic** | **Key Vocabulary** |
                | --- | --- |
                | **Education** | curriculum, tuition, scholarship, academic, literacy, compulsory, tertiary |
                | **Technology** | innovation, automation, digital literacy, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity |
                | **Environment** | sustainability, carbon emissions, renewable energy, biodiversity, deforestation |
                | **Health** | pandemic, sedentary lifestyle, mental health, obesity, healthcare system |
                | **Society** | inequality, globalisation, urbanisation, cultural heritage, social cohesion |
                | **Crime** | rehabilitation, deterrent, recidivism, incarceration, juvenile delinquency |

    - Opinion Essay
        - Content
            ## When you see the question "To what extent do you agree or disagree?"
            
            This is the most common Task 2 question. You must decide your position: do you fully agree, fully disagree, or partially agree?
            
            ## Two Approaches
            
            1. **Fully Agree/Disagree (One-sided):** This is generally easier to structure and write clearly. You pick one side and defend it with two strong reasons.
            2. **Partially Agree (Balanced):** This is more complex but can show nuance. You acknowledge the merits of both sides but ultimately lean towards one.
            
            - Task Achievement
                | Part | Fully Agree | Partially Agree |
                | --- | --- | --- |
                | Introduction | Paraphrase + "I strongly agree that..." | Paraphrase + "While I agree that X, I also believe Y" |
                | Body 1 | First reason you agree + example | The side you agree with more + example |
                | Body 2 | Second reason you agree + example | The other side (concede) + example |
                | Conclusion | Restate agreement | Restate balanced position |
            - Grammar + Coherence & Cohesion
                | **Template** | **Example** |
                | --- | --- |
                | I strongly agree/disagree that [topic] because [reason]. | I strongly agree that university education should be free because it promotes social equality. |
                | The main reason I hold this view is that [argument]. | The main reason I hold this view is that free education reduces financial barriers for low-income families. |
                | [Concession], I still believe that [main position]. | Although some argue this would burden taxpayers, I still believe that the long-term benefits outweigh the costs. |
                | A compelling example of this is [example]. | A compelling example of this is the Scandinavian model, where free tertiary education has led to higher literacy rates. |
            - Lexical Resource
                | **Intensity** | **Agree** | **Disagree** |
                | --- | --- | --- |
                | Strong | I firmly/strongly believe, I am convinced | I completely/strongly disagree, I am firmly opposed |
                | Moderate | I agree to a large extent, I generally agree | I tend to disagree, I partially disagree |
                | Balanced | While I lean towards agreement, I acknowledge... | Although there is some merit, I maintain... |

    - Discussion Essay
        - Content
            ## Recognizing the Discussion Essay
            
            Look for the trigger phrase: "Discuss both views and give your own opinion."
            
            ## The Golden Rule
            
            You **MUST** present BOTH sides of the argument fairly, and then you MUST state your own view. A very common mistake is only discussing the side you agree with, which will severely lower your Task Achievement score.
            
            - Task Achievement
                | Part | What to Write |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introduction | Paraphrase the topic + acknowledge that both views exist + state your opinion |
                | Body 1 | First view (usually the one you DISAGREE with) + reasons + example |
                | Body 2 | Second view (usually the one you AGREE with) + reasons + example |
                | Conclusion | Restate your own opinion + brief summary |
            - Grammar + Coherence & Cohesion
                | **Function** | **Template** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introducing View 1 | On the one hand, some people argue that... / It is argued by some that... |
                | Introducing View 2 | On the other hand, others believe that... / Conversely, many maintain that... |
                | Giving your opinion | In my opinion, / From my perspective, / I firmly believe that... |
                | Explaining a view | The primary reason for this view is... / Proponents of this idea argue that... |
            - Lexical Resource
                | **Concept** | **Phrases** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Supporters | proponents, advocates, supporters, those in favour of |
                | Opponents | opponents, critics, those who oppose, detractors |
                | Benefits | advantages, merits, benefits, positive aspects |
                | Drawbacks | disadvantages, drawbacks, negative consequences, downsides |

    - Problem & Solution
        - Content
            ## Recognizing Problem & Solution Essays
            
            Look for prompts like: "What are the problems does this cause?" or "What solutions can you suggest?" Sometimes it asks for "causes" instead of "problems".
            
            ## Essay Structure
            
            The structure is very straightforward: dedicate one body paragraph to the problems/causes, and the next body paragraph to the solutions. Your solutions should ideally address the specific problems you raised.
            
            - Task Achievement
                | Part | What to Write |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introduction | Paraphrase the topic + mention that there are clear problems but viable solutions exist |
                | Body 1 | 2-3 specific problems (or causes) with explanations |
                | Body 2 | 2-3 specific, realistic solutions (matching the problems) |
                | Conclusion | Summary of main points + a final thought or call to action |
            - Grammar + Coherence & Cohesion
                | **Function** | **Phrases** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introducing Problems | One of the primary causes of this issue is... / A major problem is... |
                | Adding Problems | Another significant contributing factor is... / Furthermore, this leads to... |
                | Introducing Solutions | To tackle this issue, / One effective measure would be to... |
                | Suggesting Actions | Governments should implement... / It is crucial that individuals take steps to... |
            - Lexical Resource
                | **Category** | **Vocabulary** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Causes | root cause, primary factor, driving force, underlying reason |
                | Effects/Problems | severe consequences, detrimental impact, negative repercussions, exacerbate |
                | Solutions | viable solution, effective measure, implement policies, alleviate the problem, tackle |

    - Advantages & Disadvantages
        - Content
            ## Recognizing the Prompt
            
            Look for: "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?" or simply "Discuss the advantages and disadvantages."
            
            If it asks if they "outweigh," you MUST take a clear position.
            
            ## How to Structure
            
            Discuss the advantages in one paragraph, and the disadvantages in the other. It is usually best to put the side you think is STRONGER in Body 1, but either order works as long as you are clear.
            
            - Task Achievement
                | Part | What to Write |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introduction | Paraphrase + state clearly which side outweighs the other |
                | Body 1 | Advantages (with examples) |
                | Body 2 | Disadvantages (with examples) |
                | Conclusion | Restate which side is stronger + brief summary |
            - Grammar + Coherence & Cohesion
                | **Function** | **Templates** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introducing Advantages | There are several notable advantages to... / The most significant benefit is... |
                | Introducing Disadvantages | However, there are also significant drawbacks. / A major disadvantage is... |
                | Weighing | Although there are drawbacks, the benefits far outweigh them. / The negative impacts eclipse the positive aspects. |
            - Lexical Resource
                | **Synonyms** | **Words** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Advantages | benefits, merits, positive aspects, upsides, pros |
                | Disadvantages | drawbacks, downsides, negative aspects, cons, shortcomings |
                | Outweigh | eclipse, surpass, overshadow, are more significant than |

    - Two-Part Question
        - Content
            ## Recognizing Two-Part Questions
            
            This prompt asks two distinct questions. For example: "Why is this happening? Is this a positive or negative development?"
            
            ## The Golden Rule for Two-Part Questions
            
            You must answer BOTH questions adequately. A common trap is to write a lot about the first question and barely answer the second. Give them equal weight.
            
            - Task Achievement
                | Part | What to Write |
                | --- | --- |
                | Introduction | Paraphrase the prompt + briefly answer both questions (thesis) |
                | Body 1 | Detailed answer to Question 1 + examples |
                | Body 2 | Detailed answer to Question 2 + examples |
                | Conclusion | Summarize the answers to both questions |
            - Grammar + Coherence & Cohesion
                | **Function** | **Phrases** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Addressing Q1 | Regarding the reasons behind this trend, / There are several factors contributing to... |
                | Addressing Q2 | As to whether this is positive or negative, / In terms of its impact, I believe this is a... |
                | Linking | Having considered the causes, it is important to evaluate the effects. |
            - Lexical Resource
                Since the questions vary greatly, your vocabulary must adapt. If asked about causes, use cause/effect language. If asked to evaluate (positive/negative), use evaluation language.
                
                | **Evaluation** | **Vocabulary** |
                | --- | --- |
                | Positive | beneficial, advantageous, progressive step, favourable outcome |
                | Negative | detrimental, harmful, negative consequence, adverse effect |
