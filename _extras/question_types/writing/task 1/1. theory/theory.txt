- writing + theory
    - Understanding the Task
        - Content

            IELTS Academic Writing Task 1 asks you to describe visual information — a graph, chart, table, diagram, or map — in at least **150 words** within approximately **20 minutes**.

            ## What You Must Do

            - **Describe**, not interpret or give opinions.
            - **Select and report** the key features and comparisons.
            - **Write at least 150 words** (under-length responses are penalized).
            - You are **NOT** asked to explain causes or give recommendations unless it is a process diagram.

            ## The 4 Assessment Criteria

            Your response is marked equally on four criteria, each worth 25%:

            | Criterion | What it measures |
            | --- | --- |
            | **Task Achievement (TA)** | Did you cover all key features and write 150+ words? |
            | **Coherence & Cohesion (CC)** | Is your response logically organised with good linking? |
            | **Lexical Resource (LR)** | Do you use a range of accurate vocabulary? |
            | **Grammatical Range & Accuracy (GRA)** | Do you use varied and correct grammar? |

            ## The Recommended Time Plan

            - **1–2 min:** Analyse the visual carefully.
            - **2–3 min:** Plan your structure (intro, overview, body paragraphs).
            - **13–14 min:** Write your response.
            - **1–2 min:** Check for errors.

            ## Common Beginner Mistakes

            - Copying the question prompt word-for-word (you **must paraphrase** the introduction).
            - Writing a personal opinion ("I think this chart shows…").
            - Describing every single number without selecting the most significant ones.
            - Forgetting to write an **Overview** — the most commonly missed section.

    - Paragraph Structure
        - Content

            Every high-scoring Task 1 response follows the same **4-paragraph structure**. Mastering this skeleton means you never waste time deciding how to organise your answer.

            ## The 4-Paragraph Structure

            ### 1. Introduction (1–2 sentences)
            Paraphrase what the visual shows. Do **not** copy the prompt.
            > *"The line graph illustrates changes in the average temperature of three cities over a 12-month period."*

            ### 2. Overview (2–3 sentences)
            State the **most significant overall trends** without specific numbers. This is your "bird's eye view" of the data.
            > *"Overall, City A consistently recorded the highest temperatures throughout the year, while City C remained the coldest. All three cities experienced their peak temperatures in July."*

            ### 3. Body Paragraph 1 (3–4 sentences)
            Describe the main features and key data of the **first group** (e.g., the highest values, the most prominent trend).

            ### 4. Body Paragraph 2 (3–4 sentences)
            Describe the main features and key data of the **second group** (e.g., the remaining items, comparisons, or a contrasting trend).

            ## Where to Put the Overview

            You can place the Overview **after the Introduction** (recommended) or **after the Body Paragraphs** as a final statement. Placing it after the introduction signals to the examiner immediately that you understand the data at a macro level.

            ## What NOT to Include

            - A separate "Conclusion" giving personal opinions.
            - A sentence like *"In conclusion, this data shows that society needs..."* — Task 1 does not require recommendations or a conclusion.

    - Writing the Introduction
        - Content

            The Introduction is the first thing the examiner reads. It must be well-written, concise, and — most importantly — **must paraphrase the question prompt**, never copy it.

            ## What the Introduction Does

            - Tells the examiner what type of visual is being described.
            - States what the visual shows (topic) and the timeframe or categories, if applicable.
            - Is typically **1–2 sentences**.

            ## The Paraphrase Formula

            Swap out as many words as possible using synonyms and restructure the sentence.

            | Original Prompt Words | Paraphrase Options |
            | --- | --- |
            | shows | illustrates / presents / provides data on / gives information about |
            | the number of | the figure for / the quantity of |
            | from 2000 to 2020 | over a 20-year period / between 2000 and 2020 |
            | percentage | proportion / share |
            | countries | nations |

            ## Example

            **Prompt:** *"The graph below shows the number of tourists visiting three countries between 2005 and 2015."*

            **Poor Introduction (copied):** *"The graph below shows the number of tourists visiting three countries between 2005 and 2015."*

            **Strong Introduction (paraphrased):** *"The line graph illustrates the figures for tourist arrivals across three nations over a ten-year period from 2005 to 2015."*

            ## Common Errors

            - Starting with "This graph shows…" without identifying the **type** of graph.
            - Including data or numbers in the introduction.
            - Using the word "below" (this refers to the question paper, not your writing).

    - Writing the Overview
        - Content

            The Overview is the **most important paragraph** in your Task 1 response. Without it, you cannot score above Band 5 for Task Achievement. It requires you to zoom out and identify the **2 biggest trends** in the data without referencing specific numbers.

            ## What Makes a Good Overview

            A strong overview:
            - Identifies the **overall trend or the most dominant feature** (e.g., which item was highest, what the general direction was).
            - Contains **a second major observation** (e.g., which was lowest, what stands out overall).
            - Uses **no specific numbers or dates**.
            - Is **2–3 sentences** in length.
            - Begins with a signpost: *"Overall, …"* or *"In general, …"*

            ## How to Identify What to Write

            Ask yourself these questions when looking at the visual:
            1. What is the single biggest story? (e.g., "sales rose dramatically")
            2. Is there a clear winner/loser? (e.g., "Product A always dominated")
            3. Is there a noticeable exception or reversal? (e.g., "all fell except Category C")

            ## Example

            **Chart:** A bar chart showing coffee consumption in 5 countries in 2022. Finland has the highest consumption, France the lowest, and all countries show above-average consumption except France.

            **Weak Overview:** *"Finland consumed 12 kg per person, while France consumed only 5 kg."* ❌ (Contains numbers — belongs in the body.)

            **Strong Overview:** *"Overall, Finland recorded by far the highest coffee consumption among the five countries, while France was the only nation to fall below the average level."* ✓

    - Describing Line Graphs
        - Content

            Line graphs are the **most common chart type** in IELTS Writing Task 1. They show how one or more values **change over time**. Your job is to describe those changes accurately using a varied range of trend language.

            ## Key Vocabulary for Trends

            ### Verbs (for upward movement)
            rise, increase, grow, climb, surge, rocket, jump

            ### Verbs (for downward movement)
            fall, decrease, decline, drop, plummet, dip, sink

            ### Verbs (for no change)
            remain stable, stay constant, level off, plateau, fluctuate (around a value)

            ### Adverbs (modifying the speed/degree)
            sharply, dramatically, significantly, rapidly, steadily, gradually, slightly, marginally

            ### Noun Forms (for variety)
            - *There was a sharp rise in…*
            - *Sales experienced a gradual decline…*
            - *The figure recorded a significant increase…*

            ## Describing Starting and Ending Points

            Always specify where a value **began** and where it **ended**:
            > *"In 2000, the number of users stood at 5 million. By 2020, this figure had risen sharply to 45 million."*

            ## Describing Special Features

            - **Peak:** *"Sales reached a peak of 80 units in March."*
            - **Trough/lowest point:** *"The value hit a trough of 12% in 2010."*
            - **Plateau:** *"Growth levelled off at around 60% between 2015 and 2018."*

    - Describing Bar Charts
        - Content

            Bar charts display **comparisons** between categories, either at a single point in time or across multiple time periods. Your task is to **compare and contrast** the key values rather than simply listing all the numbers.

            ## Key Language for Comparing

            ### Expressing the Highest/Lowest
            - *"X had the highest / largest / greatest figure."*
            - *"Y recorded the lowest / smallest proportion."*
            - *"X accounted for the most / least."*

            ### Comparatives and Superlatives
            - *"Country A consumed significantly more than Country B."*
            - *"The youngest age group was by far the most active."*

            ### Expressing Fractions and Proportions
            - *"X was roughly double / triple the figure for Y."*
            - *"Y was approximately half the amount of X."*
            - *"X was around four times greater than Y."*

            ## Strategy for Bar Charts: Group and Compare

            Do NOT describe each bar one by one. Instead, **group similar bars** and compare them:

            **Weak (listing):** *"Men scored 60. Women scored 75. Elderly men scored 45. Elderly women scored 55."*

            **Strong (grouping):** *"Women consistently recorded higher scores than their male counterparts across both age groups. Among younger respondents, the gap was 15 points, narrowing to 10 points in the elderly group."*

            ## Useful Sentence Starters
            - *"By contrast, …"*
            - *"While X showed the highest value, Y remained considerably lower at…"*
            - *"In comparison, …"*

    - Describing Pie Charts
        - Content

            Pie charts show **parts of a whole**, where all segments add up to **100%**. Your task is to describe the **proportions** and make comparisons between segments — not to list every number mechanically.

            ## Key Vocabulary for Proportions

            ### Expressing Large Proportions
            *account for / represent / make up / constitute + a large/significant/dominant share/proportion/percentage*

            > *"Transport accounted for the largest share at 35%."*
            > *"Housing represented over a third of total expenditure."*

            ### Expressing Small Proportions
            *account for / make up a small/minor/negligible share*

            > *"Entertainment constituted a relatively minor proportion at just 8%."*

            ### Expressing Fractions (avoid overusing percentages)
            - nearly a quarter / approximately a third / just over half / almost two-thirds

            ## Strategy: Compare the Biggest and Smallest, Then the Middle

            1. **Start with the dominant segment** (largest slice).
            2. **Follow with the smallest segment(s).**
            3. **Group the middle segments** together if they are similar in size.

            ## Common Mistakes

            - **Listing all segments:** *"A is 35%, B is 25%, C is 20%, D is 12%, E is 8%."* — This is mechanical and gets low marks.
            - **Ignoring fractions:** Always try to express percentages also as fractions where possible (35% ≈ just over a third).
            - **Forgetting to compare:** Always contrast the biggest with the smallest.

    - Describing Tables
        - Content

            Tables in IELTS Writing Task 1 contain **multiple categories and multiple values**. They can show data across several groups and time periods simultaneously, making them the most data-rich visual you could receive.

            ## Reading the Table Strategically

            Before writing, spend 1–2 minutes scanning the table:
            1. **Read the title** — this tells you the overall topic.
            2. **Read the row and column headers** — these tell you the categories being compared.
            3. **Find the highest and lowest values** — these are your key features.
            4. **Notice any interesting patterns** — values that stand out, big gaps, or surprising similarities.

            ## What to Select

            You cannot and should not describe every cell. Select:
            - The **highest** value(s) overall.
            - The **lowest** value(s) overall.
            - Notable **comparisons** (e.g., one country significantly higher than another).
            - Any **exceptional pattern** (e.g., one row that behaves differently from all others).

            ## Useful Language for Tables

            - *"Country A recorded the highest figure for X at [value]."*
            - *"In contrast, Country E had the lowest value across all categories."*
            - *"The data for Y remained relatively consistent across the five groups."*
            - *"Interestingly, the figures for Z showed the greatest variation."*

        - Quiz
            1. What is the most important first step when approaching a table in Task 1?
            A) Immediately start writing the introduction.
            B) Read the title and row/column headers to understand what is being compared.
            C) Count the total number of cells in the table.
            D) Add up all the numbers to find the total.

            Hint: Understanding the structure comes before writing.
            Answer: B
            Why: The title and headers tell you the topic and categories. Without reading them carefully, you risk misunderstanding what the table actually shows and writing about the wrong things.

            1. A table has 5 rows and 6 columns, giving 30 individual data points. How many should you attempt to describe?
            A) All 30 — the examiner wants complete coverage.

            Hint: Task 1 is about selecting key features, not data dumping.
            Answer: C
            Why: The IELTS marking criteria reward "selecting key features." Describing all 30 values would result in a very mechanical, low-scoring response. You must demonstrate judgment in selecting what matters most.

            Answer: B

    - Describing Processes
        - Content

            Process diagrams appear less frequently than charts but are very distinctive because they show **a sequence of steps** — either a natural cycle (e.g., the water cycle) or a man-made manufacturing process (e.g., how paper is made).

            ## Key Differences from Chart Tasks

            | Chart Tasks | Process Tasks |
            | --- | --- |
            | Compare and contrast data values | Describe each step in sequence |
            | Use trend language (rise, fall) | Use process language (is produced, enters, moves to) |
            | Select key features | Cover ALL stages — nothing should be omitted |
            | No chronological order required | Must follow the correct sequence |

            ## Essential Language for Processes

            ### Sequencing Words
            First / To begin with / Initially / At the first stage
            Then / Next / After this / Subsequently / Following this
            Finally / At the last stage / Once [step] is complete, …

            ### Passive Voice (critical for man-made processes)
            *"The raw material **is fed** into the machine."*
            *"The mixture **is then heated** to 200 degrees."*
            *"The product **is packaged** and **distributed** to retailers."*

            ### Active Voice (for natural cycles)
            *"Water **evaporates** from the surface of the ocean."*
            *"Clouds **form** and **release** precipitation."*

            ## What the Overview Should Include for a Process

            State the number of stages and whether it is a natural cycle or a man-made process:
            > *"Overall, the process involves six distinct stages, beginning with the extraction of raw materials and ending with the distribution of the finished product."*

        - Quiz
            1. What is the most important difference between describing a process and describing a bar chart?
            A) Processes are shorter to write than charts.
            B) For processes, you must cover ALL stages in sequence, whereas for charts you select key features.
            C) Processes require personal opinions.
            D) Bar charts use passive voice while processes use active voice.

            Hint: Every step in a process matters — you cannot skip stages.
            Answer: B
            Why: Unlike charts where you select the most significant data, process diagrams require you to describe every stage in the correct order. Missing a stage would directly lose you marks under Task Achievement.

            1. Which sentence correctly uses the passive voice to describe a manufacturing step?
            A) "Workers are heating the mixture."
            B) "The mixture heated to 200°C."
            C) "The mixture is heated to 200°C."
            D) "Heating occurs the mixture at 200°C."

            Hint: Passive voice = to be + past participle.
            Answer: C
            Why: The correct passive structure is "to be (is/are) + past participle." "The mixture is heated" correctly follows this structure. Options A (active, not passive), B (missing auxiliary verb), and D (ungrammatical) are all incorrect.

            1. Which word is most appropriate to describe the first step in a process?
            A) "Finally"
            B) "Subsequently"
            C) "Initially"
            D) "As a result"

            Hint: You need a word that signals the very beginning.
            Answer: C
            Why: "Initially" or "First" or "To begin with" are used to introduce the first stage of a process. "Subsequently" and "Finally" are for middle and last stages. "As a result" implies cause and effect rather than sequence.

            1. A natural cycle like the water cycle — which voice is more appropriate?
            A) Always passive voice
            B) Always active voice
            C) Active voice, since natural processes happen without human agency
            D) Either voice is equally appropriate

            Hint: Is it humans or nature performing the action?
            Answer: C
            Why: Natural cycles use active voice because no human agent is involved — the subject (water, clouds, the sun) performs the action itself: "Water evaporates," "Clouds form." Passive voice is reserved for man-made processes where the agent (workers, machines) is less important than the action.

            1. What should the Overview of a process description include?
            A) The most significant steps only
            B) The number of stages and whether it is a natural or man-made process
            C) All the specific details and measurements
            D) The writer's opinion on the efficiency of the process

            Hint: An overview gives the big picture — total stages and general nature first.
            Answer: B
            Why: The overview for a process should state the total number of stages and the overall nature of the process (natural cycle vs man-made), e.g., "Overall, the process consists of six stages, beginning with... and culminating in..." This gives the examiner the macro-level view before the detail.

    - Describing Maps
        - Content

            Map tasks ask you to describe **how a location has changed over two time periods**, or to **compare two different plans** of the same area. They test your ability to use **location language** and **language of change**.

            ## Two Types of Map Tasks

            ### Type 1: Change Over Time (e.g., a village in 1950 vs. 2020)
            Focus on:
            - What has been **added** (new buildings, roads, facilities).
            - What has been **removed** or **replaced**.
            - What has **remained the same**.

            ### Type 2: Comparing Two Plans (e.g., two different development proposals)
            Focus on:
            - Similarities between the plans.
            - Key differences in the placement of features.

            ## Essential Location Language

            - *"in the north/south/east/west/centre of the map"*
            - *"to the north of / adjacent to / next to / opposite"*
            - *"in the northern/southern corner"*

            ## Language of Change

            | Meaning | Expression |
            | --- | --- |
            | Something new was built | "A hotel was constructed / built / added" |
            | Something was removed | "The forest was cleared / demolished / removed" |
            | Something was converted | "The farmland was converted into a car park" |
            | Nothing changed | "The church remained / was retained / stayed the same" |

        - Quiz
            1. A map shows a town in 1990 and 2024. What is the main task you must perform?
            A) Describe only the 2024 map.
            B) Give your opinion about which version of the town is better.
            C) Describe how the town has changed between 1990 and 2024, including additions, removals, and unchanged features.
            D) List all the buildings present in both maps.

            Hint: Change over time is the core theme.
            Answer: C
            Why: You must address all three types of change: what was added (new features), what was removed (demolitions), and what remained. Describing only one map or giving opinions would lose you marks.

            1. Which phrase correctly describes that a building was demolished?
            A) "The school was built in the south."
            B) "The hospital was retained."
            C) "The old factory was demolished and replaced by a shopping centre."
            D) "A park remained in the east."

            Hint: Demolished = knocked down / removed.
            Answer: C
            Why: "Was demolished and replaced by" correctly uses the passive voice to describe a specific type of change (removal + replacement). This demonstrates the language of change required for map tasks.

            1. Which phrase correctly locates a feature on a map?
            A) "The library is very far from things."
            B) "The library is in the north-eastern corner of the town, adjacent to the main road."
            C) "There is a library somewhere around the map."
            D) "A library exists near some buildings."

            Hint: Location language requires precise directional phrases.
            Answer: B
            Why: "In the north-eastern corner" specifies the exact location, and "adjacent to the main road" provides a reference point. Vague phrases like "very far" or "somewhere" are not acceptable in formal academic writing.

            1. In a map task, a river appears in both the 1980 and 2020 versions. How would you describe this?
            A) "The river was added in 2020."
            B) "The river was demolished."
            C) "The river remained unchanged / was retained throughout the period."
            D) "A new river appeared in 1980."

            Hint: Unchanged features need specific vocabulary too.
            Answer: C
            Why: "Remained unchanged" or "was retained" are the correct expressions for features that stayed the same between two maps. "Added" and "demolished" imply change, which is incorrect.

            1. Which overview is most appropriate for a map task showing a village in 1970 vs. 2020?
            A) "Overall, the village experienced significant development over the 50-year period, with considerable expansion of residential and commercial areas replacing much of the original farmland."
            B) "Overall, there are many buildings in the village."
            C) "I think the 2020 village is much better than the 1970 version."
            D) "Overall, the map shows a village."

            Hint: The overview should summarise the scale and type of change.
            Answer: A
            Why: A strong overview for a map task identifies the overall scale of change (significant development) and the dominant transformation (farmland → residential/commercial). It avoids specific details, opinions, and vague descriptions.

    - Language for Data
        - Content

            Accurately referencing **numbers, units, and approximations** is a fundamental skill in Task 1. Using a variety of expressions rather than repeating the same phrase shows lexical resource and grammatical range.

            ## Introducing Figures

            | Expression | Example |
            | --- | --- |
            | stood at | *"In 2010, the figure stood at 45%."* |
            | was recorded at | *"The value was recorded at 3.2 million."* |
            | reached | *"Production reached 8,000 units per day."* |
            | amounted to | *"Total expenditure amounted to $2.4 billion."* |

            ## Approximation Language (avoid too many exact numbers)

            - **"approximately / around / roughly / about"** → *"approximately 60%"*
            - **"just over / slightly above"** → *"just over a quarter"*
            - **"just under / slightly below"** → *"just under half"*
            - **"nearly / almost"** → *"nearly 80%"*

            ## Expressing Units Correctly

            | Unit | Correct Use |
            | --- | --- |
            | % | *"45 percent"* or *"45%"* |
            | Millions | *"3.5 million"* (not "3.5 millions") |
            | Thousands | *"12,000"* or *"12 thousand"* |
            | USD/GBP | *"$45 billion"* or *"45 billion dollars"* |

            ## Avoiding Repetition

            Do not write "the number increased, the number then decreased, the number then…"

            Use pronouns and noun substitutions:
            - *"this figure"*, *"the value"*, *"the proportion"*, *"the statistic"*, *"the rate"*

        - Quiz
            1. Which phrase correctly introduces a data value?
            A) "The number is at 45%."
            B) "In 2010, the figure stood at 45%."
            C) "45% was the thing in 2010."
            D) "There was 45% in year 2010."

            Hint: "Stood at" is one of the standard academic expressions for presenting data.
            Answer: B
            Why: "Stood at" is a formal academic phrase for describing a value at a specific point in time. Options A ("is at"), C, and D are all grammatically awkward or informal.

            1. How would you express "43%" using approximation language to add variety?
            A) "43 percents"
            B) "Forty-and-three percent"
            C) "Almost half"
            D) "More than a lot"

            Hint: 43% ≈ nearly half.
            Answer: C
            Why: 43% is close to 50% (half), so "almost half" or "just under half" is an appropriate approximation. This demonstrates lexical resource by avoiding excessive repetition of exact percentage figures.

            A) "millions" should be "million" when preceded by a number.
            B) "people" should be "persons."
            C) "3.5" should be written as "three point five."
            D) There is no error.

            Answer: A
            Why: In English, "million", "billion", "thousand", and "hundred" do NOT take an 's' when preceded by a number: "3.5 million" (not "3.5 millions"). The plural form with 's' is only used without a preceding number: "millions of people."

            Why: "Just under half" means slightly below 50%. "Just over half" would mean slightly above 50%. These precise approximation phrases are important for accurately describing values that are close to convenient fractions.

    - Language for Change
        - Content

            One of the most common lexical errors in Task 1 is using **only verb forms** or **repeating the same verb** to describe change. High-scoring responses alternate between **verb forms** and **noun forms** of the same concept.

            ## Verb ↔ Noun Pairs

            | Verb | Noun |
            | --- | --- |
            | increase | an increase in / a rise in |
            | decrease / fall / drop | a decrease in / a fall in / a drop in |
            | rise | a rise in |
            | grow | growth in |
            | fluctuate | fluctuation in |
            | decline | a decline in |

            ## Using Verb Forms

            *"Sales **rose** sharply between 2010 and 2015."*
            *"The unemployment rate **fell** gradually over the decade."*

            ## Using Noun Forms

            *"There was a **sharp rise** in sales between 2010 and 2015."*
            *"The decade saw a **gradual fall** in the unemployment rate."*

            ## Adjective / Adverb Pairs

            The adjective modifies the noun; the adverb modifies the verb:

            | Adjective (with noun) | Adverb (with verb) |
            | --- | --- |
            | a **sharp** increase | increased **sharply** |
            | a **dramatic** fall | fell **dramatically** |
            | a **gradual** decline | declined **gradually** |
            | a **slight** rise | rose **slightly** |

        - Quiz
            1. Convert this sentence to noun form: "Profits fell dramatically in 2019."
            A) "Profits were falling dramatically in 2019."
            B) "There was a dramatic fall in profits in 2019."
            C) "Profits had a dramatic in 2019."
            D) "2019 saw profits fell dramatically."

            Hint: Noun form = "There was a [adjective] [noun] in [subject] in [year]."
            Answer: B
            Why: The correct noun form uses "There was a + adjective (dramatic) + noun (fall) + in + subject (profits) + in + time." Option D has a grammar error (saw + bare infinitive cannot follow "fell"), and C is ungrammatical.

            1. Which sentence correctly pairs an adverb with a verb of change?
            A) "Sales rose sharp."
            B) "There was a sharply rise."
            C) "Sales rose sharply."
            D) "Sharply, the rise in sales."

            Hint: Adverbs modify verbs — "rose + adverb."
            Answer: C
            Why: Adverbs (sharply, gradually, dramatically) modify verbs directly: "rose sharply." "Sharp" in option A is an adjective, not an adverb. Option B incorrectly uses an adverb to modify a noun.

            1. A student writes: "Exports increased. Imports decreased. Profits increased. Taxes decreased." What is the main problem?
            A) The sentences are too long.
            B) The student only uses two verbs (increased/decreased), creating repetitive and low-scoring writing.
            C) The facts are incorrect.
            D) There are no linking words.

            Hint: Variety of vocabulary is key to Lexical Resource.
            Answer: B
            Why: Repeating "increased" and "decreased" throughout the response demonstrates a limited lexical range. A high-scoring response would use: rose, grew, surged, climbed (for increases) and fell, dropped, declined, dipped (for decreases).

            1. Which sentence correctly uses the noun form of "grow"?
            A) "There was a significant growth in tourism."
            B) "Tourism significantly growth."
            C) "Tourism had growth significantly."
            D) "A significantly growth occurred."

            Hint: "Growth" is always a noun — check what structure it needs.
            Answer: A
            Why: "There was a [adjective] growth in [noun]" is the correct noun structure. Note that unlike most change nouns, "growth" is used without the article in some structures: "growth in [subject]."

            1. "The rate experienced a slight dip in 2008 before recovering." What does "a slight dip" mean?
            A) A very large and sudden fall
            B) A minor and temporary decrease
            C) A complete collapse
            D) A rapid increase

            Hint: "Slight" = small; "dip" = a brief downward movement.
            Answer: B
            Why: A "dip" refers to a small, temporary downward movement — not a sustained decline. Combined with "slight," it indicates the fall was both minor in size and brief in duration. Understanding these nuanced vocabulary items is important for both reading and writing Task 1.

    - Coherence and Cohesion
        - Content

            Coherence & Cohesion (CC) is one of the four marking criteria and accounts for **25% of your score**. It evaluates how **logically organised** your response is and how effectively you **connect your ideas** using linking language.

            ## Two Components

            ### Coherence — Logical Organisation
            Your paragraphs must have a clear purpose and logical sequence:
            - Introduction → what the visual shows
            - Overview → biggest trends
            - Body 1 → first group of data
            - Body 2 → second group of data

            Each paragraph should have **one main focus** and flow logically from the paragraph before it.

            ### Cohesion — Connecting Language
            Use a variety of referencing and linking devices:

            #### Linking Words (Addition)
            Furthermore / Moreover / In addition / Also

            #### Linking Words (Contrast)
            However / By contrast / On the other hand / While / Whereas / Although

            #### Linking Words (Sequence — for processes)
            First / Then / Subsequently / Finally / After this

            #### Referencing (avoid repeating the same noun)
            *"Sales rose in 2010. **This figure** then declined in 2011."*
            *"Country A had the highest value. **The nation** also showed..."*

        - Quiz
            1. What does "coherence" refer to in IELTS marking?
            A) The use of a wide vocabulary range.
            B) The logical organisation of ideas within and across paragraphs.
            C) The accuracy of grammar.
            D) The correct use of linking words only.

            Hint: Coherence = "does this make logical sense as a whole?"
            Answer: B
            Why: Coherence is about the overall logical flow and organisation of your writing — whether ideas progress sensibly from one paragraph to the next. Linking words contribute to cohesion, while vocabulary is a separate criterion (Lexical Resource).

            1. A student writes two contrasting ideas without any linking word: "Country A had the highest sales. Country B had the lowest sales." How can this be improved?
            A) "Country A had the highest sales, furthermore Country B had the lowest sales."
            B) "Country A had the highest sales, whereas Country B recorded the lowest figures."
            C) "Country A and Country B both had sales."
            D) "Country A had the highest sales. In conclusion, Country B had the lowest."

            Hint: You need a contrast connector between two opposite facts.
            Answer: B
            Why: "Whereas" correctly introduces a contrast between two opposite facts in the same sentence. "Furthermore" signals addition (not contrast), and "In conclusion" is not appropriate inside a body paragraph.

            1. Which of the following is an example of referencing to avoid noun repetition?
            A) Replacing "the sales figure" with "sales figure" (dropping "the").
            B) Replacing "Country A" with "this nation" or "the country" later in the paragraph.
            C) Repeating "Country A" in every sentence.
            D) Using "it" for every noun in the paragraph.

            Hint: Referencing uses pronouns and noun substitutes to point back to an already-mentioned subject.
            Answer: B
            Why: "This nation" or "the country" are cohesive devices that refer back to "Country A" mentioned earlier. Repeating the same noun every sentence drops your CC score, while using only "it" for all nouns is ambiguous and unclear.

            1. Which linking word is most appropriate to add a secondary supporting point?
            A) "However"
            B) "Therefore"
            C) "Furthermore"
            D) "Although"

            Hint: You need a word that adds to an existing point (not contrast or cause/effect).
            Answer: C
            Why: "Furthermore" and "Moreover" are used to add supplementary supporting information. "However" and "Although" signal contrast, while "Therefore" signals a consequence — neither of which is appropriate for simple data addition.

            1. What is the main risk of using too many linking words in Task 1?
            A) It makes the response too long.
            B) Overusing or misusing linking words can actually lower your CC score if they are forced or incorrect.
            C) Too many linking words make the vocabulary more repetitive.
            D) There is no risk; more linking words always improve scores.

            Hint: Quality over quantity.
            Answer: B
            Why: The examiner checks whether linking words are used accurately and appropriately. Starting every sentence with "Furthermore," "Moreover," or "However" without a genuine logical reason to do so is penalised as mechanical use of cohesive devices.

    - Lexical Resource
        - Content

            Lexical Resource (LR) measures the **range, accuracy, and appropriacy** of your vocabulary. It is worth 25% of your Task 1 mark. High-scoring responses show that you can express the same idea in multiple ways and use academic, precise vocabulary.

            ## Key LR Strategies

            ### 1. Avoid Word Repetition
            Never use the same key word more than twice in a paragraph:
            - ❌ *"increased… increased… increased…"*
            - ✓ *"rose… grew… saw a climb… experienced a surge…"*

            ### 2. Paraphrase the Prompt Vocabulary
            Use synonyms when referring back to the topic:
            - "countries" → nations / states
            - "amount" → quantity / volume / figure
            - "shows" → illustrates / presents / depicts

            ### 3. Use Collocation Correctly
            Some words naturally appear together (collocations):
            - ✓ *"a sharp rise"* (not a "heavy rise")
            - ✓ *"a significant drop"* (not a "big fall of")
            - ✓ *"reach a peak"* (not "arrive at a peak")

            ### 4. Spelling and Word Form
            - Misspelling a word **reduces your LR score**.
            - Using the wrong form of a word (e.g., writing "dramatically" instead of "dramatic" with a noun) also reduces your score.

        - Quiz
            1. A student uses the word "increased" four times in one paragraph. Which revision best improves Lexical Resource?
            A) Replace every "increased" with "went up."
            B) Use a variety: "rose," "grew," "climbed," and "saw a surge."
            C) Delete two of them and keep two.
            D) Add "very" before each "increased."

            Hint: Range is rewarded, not repetition of a single synonym.
            Answer: B
            Why: Lexical Resource rewards the use of a wide range of vocabulary. Using four different synonyms ("rose," "grew," "climbed," "saw a surge") demonstrates this range far better than replacing one repeated word with another repeated synonym.

            1. Which is the correct collocation?
            A) "A heavy rise in sales"
            B) "A sharp rise in sales"
            C) "An intensive rise in sales"
            D) "A strong rise in sales"

            Hint: Only one of these adjectives naturally pairs with "rise" in academic English.
            Answer: B
            Why: "A sharp rise" is the established academic collocation. "Heavy," "intensive," and "strong" do not naturally collocate with "rise" in this context, and using them would lower your Lexical Resource mark.

            1. A student writes: "The figure dramatically increased." What subtle error have they made?
            A) "dramatically" should be "dramatic" because "increase" is a verb.
            B) No error has been made.
            C) "increased" should be "increasing."
            D) "dramatically" can never be used with "increased."

            Hint: Is "dramatically" the right word form here?
            Answer: B
            Why: Actually this sentence IS correct — "dramatically" (adverb) correctly modifies the verb "increased." However, if the sentence were "There was a dramatically increase," then the error would be using an adverb ("dramatically") instead of an adjective ("dramatic") to modify the noun "increase."

            A) "Place" or "area"
            B) "Nation" or "state"
            C) "Land" or "spot"
            D) "Territory" or "zone"

            Answer: B
            Why: "Nation" and "state" are the standard academic synonyms for "country" in formal writing. "Place," "area," "land," "spot," "territory," and "zone" either carry different meanings or are too informal/imprecise for Task 1.

            1. A student spells "approximately" as "aproximatly." How does this affect their score?
            A) No effect — spelling is not assessed in Task 1.

            C) It lowers their Task Achievement score.

            Answer: B
            Why: The IELTS marking guide explicitly states that spelling errors reduce Lexical Resource. Consistently misspelling words signals a limited command of English vocabulary and reduces your LR band score.

    - Grammatical Range and Accuracy
        - Content

            Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA) accounts for **25% of your Task 1 mark**. It measures both how **varied** your sentence structures are and how **accurately** you use grammar.

            ## Range — Use a Variety of Structures

            ### Simple Sentences
            *"Sales rose sharply in 2010."*

            ### Compound Sentences (joining two clauses)
            *"Sales rose sharply in 2010, and they continued to climb until 2015."*

            ### Complex Sentences (subordinate clauses)
            *"Although sales rose sharply in 2010, they experienced a decline the following year."*
            *"While Country A maintained the highest figures, Country B consistently recorded the lowest values."*

            ### Relative Clauses
            *"Country A, which had the highest figures throughout the period, also showed the most rapid growth."*

            ## Accuracy — Common Errors to Avoid

            | Error | Incorrect | Correct |
            | --- | --- | --- |
            | Subject–verb agreement | "The data shows..." | "The data show..." or "The data shows..." (both accepted) |
            | Article misuse | "The biggest increase was in a 2015" | "The biggest increase was in 2015" |
            | Tense consistency | Mixing present and past for the same period | Use past simple for completed time periods |
            | Preposition errors | "increased of 20%" | "increased by 20%" |

            ## Describing Change — Correct Prepositions

            - Increased **by** 20% (= the amount of change)
            - Increased **to** 40% (= the new value)
            - Increased **from** 20% **to** 40%

        - Quiz
            1. Which sentence demonstrates the highest grammatical range?
            A) "Sales went up. Imports went down. Profits went up."
            B) "While sales and profits both rose considerably, imports declined sharply over the same period."
            C) "Sales and imports and profits all changed."
            D) "Sales go up imports go down profits go up."

            Hint: Complex sentences with subordinate clauses demonstrate higher GRA.
            Answer: B
            Why: Option B uses a complex sentence with a "While" subordinate clause to contrast two trends in one well-structured sentence. This demonstrates grammatical range. Options A and C use only simple sentences, and D has run-on errors.

            1. Which sentence correctly uses prepositions to describe change?
            A) "Sales increased of 30%."
            B) "Sales increased with 30%."
            C) "Sales increased by 30%."
            D) "Sales increased for 30%."

            Hint: The amount of change uses a specific preposition.
            Answer: C
            Why: "Increased by [amount]" is the correct preposition for describing the magnitude of change (how much it went up). "Increased to [value]" describes the new level reached. "Increased of/with/for" are all incorrect collocations.

            1. What tense should you use when describing a bar chart showing data for the year 2015?
            A) Present simple ("Sales are 45%")
            B) Future perfect ("Sales will have been 45%")
            C) Past simple ("Sales stood at 45%")
            D) Present perfect ("Sales have stood at 45%")

            Hint: 2015 is a completed, fixed time period in the past.
            Answer: C
            Why: Data from a specific past year is described using the past simple tense ("stood at," "reached," "accounted for"). Present simple is only used for charts without a specific time period (e.g., a general process diagram).

            1. A student writes: "Although sales rose sharply in 2010." What is wrong with this sentence?
            A) "Although" is the wrong conjunction.
            B) "Although" introduces a subordinate clause that cannot stand alone — it needs a main clause.
            C) "sharply" is in the wrong position.
            D) "rose" should be "risen."

            Hint: Subordinate clauses need a main clause to be grammatically complete.
            Answer: B
            Why: "Although" is a subordinating conjunction that begins a dependent clause. A dependent clause cannot stand alone as a complete sentence. It must be joined to an independent main clause: "Although sales rose sharply in 2010, they declined the following year."

            1. When describing a change from 20% to 45%, which sentence is grammatically complete and accurate?
            A) "Sales increased from 20% to 45%."
            B) "Sales increased from 20% of 45%."
            C) "Sales increased to 20% from 45%."
            D) "Sales increased by 20% to 45%."

            Hint: The direction is: from [starting point] to [ending point].
            Answer: A
            Why: "Increased from [lower value] to [higher value]" is the correct structure for describing movement between two data points. Option C reverses the direction, option B uses "of" (incorrect preposition), and option D conflates "by" and "to" in a way that is mathematically misleading.

        - Content

            This final lesson brings together all the skills from the previous lessons. You will learn how to **self-assess your own Task 1 writing** using the official band descriptors, identify your weaknesses, and build a targeted improvement plan.

            ## The Band Descriptor Summary

            ### Band 5
            - Task Achievement: Covers the task but may miss key features or have an unclear overview.
            - CC: Organisation is evident but inconsistent; limited use of linking devices.
            - LR: Limited vocabulary, noticeable repetition, and frequent errors.
            - GRA: Limited range of structures; frequent grammatical errors that affect communication.

            ### Band 6
            - Task Achievement: Key features are addressed; overview may be incomplete.
            - CC: Coherent organisation; some effective linking but may be mechanical.
            - LR: Adequate range of vocabulary; some errors in spelling/word form.
            - GRA: Mix of simple and complex structures; some errors but meaning is clear.

            ### Band 7
            - Task Achievement: Key features are well covered; overview is clear and present.
            - CC: Well-organised; varied use of cohesive devices.
            - LR: Sufficient range; generally accurate with only occasional errors.
            - GRA: Variety of complex structures; frequent error-free sentences.

            ## A Self-Assessment Checklist

            After every practice session, ask yourself:

            - [ ] Did I write at least 150 words?
            - [ ] Did I paraphrase the introduction (no copying)?
            - [ ] Did I write an Overview with no specific numbers?
            - [ ] Did I use varied vocabulary (verbs AND noun forms of change)?
            - [ ] Did I make comparisons rather than just listing data?
            - [ ] Did I use complex sentences (Although, While, Whereas)?
            - [ ] Did I check spelling and prepositions?

            ## How to Improve Band by Band

            | From | To | Focus |
            | --- | --- | --- |
            | Band 5 → 6 | Write an Overview; cover all key features; use basic linking words |
            | Band 6 → 7 | Use varied vocabulary; add complex sentences; ensure overview has no numbers |
            | Band 7 → 8 | Eliminate grammatical errors; use sophisticated cohesive devices; maintain consistent register |
